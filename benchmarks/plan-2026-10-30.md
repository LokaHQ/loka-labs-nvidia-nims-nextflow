pipelines. Thresholds stay as they are - the search already runs at E-value 0.1 with no

## Day 1 - unblock, and stop the standing cost (~$3)

78-minute search producing one 791 KB file every design reuses. Delete FSx, which stops
$11.20/day - optionally copying the database to S3 first (~$2) as insurance, where it

timing comparison.

Then the ablation Manuel asked for: fold one design with and without the target
alignment and compare the scores (~$0.15). It tells us what the target MSA is actually
worth before we commit to anything larger.

## Optional, if GPU capacity appears (~$20)

MSA NIM versus MMseqs2-GPU, head to head. A bonus, not a dependency - the 100-binder run
must not wait on it. Needs two 48 GB GPUs we have never once been able to schedule, plus
the GPU-format UniRef30 (~100 GB, ~$5) since our rebuilt copy is CPU-format.

**Three settings must be matched first**, or the comparison measures the wrong thing:

| Setting | Baseline (measured) | NIM (current config) |
|---|---|---|
| Databases | UniRef30 only | `all` - UniRef30 + envdb |
| E-value | 0.1 | 0.0001 |
| Max sequences | 4,324 returned | capped at 500 |

The sequence cap matters most: it would change MSA depth, which changes how OpenFold3
folds the target, which lands in `interface_dG` and `iptm`. That difference would look
like a NIM effect and would not be one. Note also `NIM_GLOBAL_MAX_MSA_DEPTH` defaults to
500 server-side, so raising the client-side flag may not be enough.
